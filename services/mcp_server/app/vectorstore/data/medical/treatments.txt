Type 2 Diabetes: Treatment and Management Guide

Overview
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic condition characterized by insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency. It accounts for approximately 90-95% of all diabetes cases worldwide.

Diagnosis
Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed based on the following criteria:
- Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)
- 2-hour plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)
- HbA1c >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- Random plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia

Lifestyle Modifications
The cornerstone of type 2 diabetes management includes:
- Medical Nutrition Therapy: A balanced diet emphasizing whole grains, vegetables, lean proteins, and healthy fats. Carbohydrate counting and portion control are essential.
- Physical Activity: At least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise, plus resistance training 2-3 times per week.
- Weight Management: A 5-7% weight loss can significantly improve glycemic control.
- Smoking Cessation: Smoking increases cardiovascular risk in diabetic patients.

Pharmacological Treatment
First-line therapy:
- Metformin: The preferred initial medication. Start at 500mg once daily, titrate to 1000mg twice daily. Reduces hepatic glucose production and improves insulin sensitivity.

Second-line agents (if HbA1c target not met after 3 months):
- GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (e.g., semaglutide, liraglutide): Preferred for patients with cardiovascular disease or obesity.
- SGLT2 Inhibitors (e.g., empagliflozin, dapagliflozin): Preferred for patients with heart failure or chronic kidney disease.
- DPP-4 Inhibitors (e.g., sitagliptin, linagliptin): Well-tolerated, weight-neutral option.
- Sulfonylureas (e.g., glipizide, glimepiride): Effective but associated with hypoglycemia and weight gain.
- Thiazolidinediones (e.g., pioglitazone): Improves insulin sensitivity but may cause fluid retention.

Insulin Therapy:
- Basal insulin (e.g., glargine, detemir) when oral agents are insufficient.
- Basal-bolus regimen for patients requiring intensified therapy.
- Typical starting dose: 0.1-0.2 units/kg/day of basal insulin.

Monitoring
- HbA1c: Every 3-6 months. Target < 7.0% for most adults.
- Self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG): Frequency depends on treatment regimen.
- Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM): Recommended for insulin-treated patients.
- Annual screening for complications: retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy.

Complications
Microvascular: Diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy.
Macrovascular: Coronary artery disease, stroke, peripheral arterial disease.
Other: Diabetic foot ulcers, infections, diabetic ketoacidosis (rare in type 2).

Cardiovascular Risk Management
- Blood pressure target: < 130/80 mmHg
- Lipid management: Statin therapy for most patients over 40
- Aspirin therapy: Consider for secondary prevention
- ACE inhibitors or ARBs: Preferred for patients with albuminuria

Hypertension Treatment Guide

Overview
Hypertension (high blood pressure) is a chronic condition affecting approximately 1.28 billion adults worldwide. It is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease, stroke, and kidney disease.

Classification
- Normal: Systolic < 120 mmHg and Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- Elevated: Systolic 120-129 mmHg and Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: Systolic 130-139 mmHg or Diastolic 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: Systolic >= 140 mmHg or Diastolic >= 90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: Systolic > 180 mmHg and/or Diastolic > 120 mmHg

Lifestyle Modifications
- DASH diet: Rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy
- Sodium restriction: < 2,300 mg/day, ideally < 1,500 mg/day
- Regular exercise: 150 minutes per week of moderate aerobic activity
- Weight loss: Each kg lost reduces BP by approximately 1 mmHg
- Alcohol moderation: Men <= 2 drinks/day, Women <= 1 drink/day
- Stress management: Meditation, deep breathing, regular sleep

Pharmacological Treatment
First-line agents:
- ACE Inhibitors (e.g., lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril): Block angiotensin-converting enzyme. Contraindicated in pregnancy.
- ARBs (e.g., losartan, valsartan, irbesartan): Alternative to ACE inhibitors. Fewer side effects like cough.
- Calcium Channel Blockers (e.g., amlodipine, nifedipine): Effective in elderly and African American patients.
- Thiazide Diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone): Low cost, effective first-line option.

Combination therapy:
- Most patients require 2 or more medications to achieve target BP.
- Common combinations: ACE inhibitor/ARB + CCB, ACE inhibitor/ARB + diuretic.
- Avoid combining ACE inhibitor + ARB (increased risk of adverse effects).

Monitoring
- Home blood pressure monitoring: Recommended for all hypertensive patients
- Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM): Gold standard for diagnosis
- Regular assessment of target organ damage: kidney function, ECG, eye examination

Common Cold and Flu Treatment Guide

Overview
The common cold and influenza (flu) are respiratory infections caused by different viruses. The common cold is typically caused by rhinoviruses, while influenza is caused by influenza A or B viruses.

Common Cold Symptoms
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Sore throat
- Cough
- Mild body aches
- Sneezing
- Low-grade fever (rare in adults)
- Generally mild symptoms lasting 7-10 days

Influenza Symptoms
- High fever (100-104°F / 38-40°C)
- Severe body aches and muscle pain
- Extreme fatigue
- Dry cough
- Headache
- Chills and sweats
- Symptoms typically more severe than common cold

Treatment for Common Cold
- Rest and adequate hydration
- Over-the-counter pain relievers: acetaminophen or ibuprofen for aches and fever
- Decongestants: pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine for nasal congestion
- Antihistamines: diphenhydramine or cetirizine for runny nose and sneezing
- Cough suppressants: dextromethorphan for dry cough
- Throat lozenges or warm salt water gargle for sore throat
- Honey (for adults and children over 1 year) for cough relief
- Saline nasal spray for congestion
- Antibiotics are NOT effective against viral infections

Treatment for Influenza
- Antiviral medications (most effective within 48 hours of symptom onset):
  - Oseltamivir (Tamiflu): 75mg twice daily for 5 days
  - Zanamivir (Relenza): Inhaled, 10mg twice daily for 5 days
  - Baloxavir (Xofluza): Single dose treatment
- Rest and hydration
- Antipyretics for fever management
- Annual influenza vaccination is the best prevention

Prevention
- Frequent hand washing with soap for at least 20 seconds
- Avoid touching face, especially eyes, nose, and mouth
- Cover coughs and sneezes with elbow or tissue
- Stay home when sick
- Annual flu vaccination (recommended for everyone 6 months and older)
- Maintain good general health: adequate sleep, balanced diet, regular exercise
